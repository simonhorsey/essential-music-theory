Blank manuscript paper and a pencil are a musician's necessities, no matter how much computers and iPads seem to be taking over the writing down of music!

One of the problems with finding blank music paper online is that lots of it has very black staff lines. This makes it difficult to see the notes you have written on top of them, especially in pencil. The paper you buy in shops tends to have thin, light, blue-grey lines so the dots are easy to read once you have put them on the staff. Mine does too.

Should you use manuscript paper or your computer?

I find that writing music down on paper first really focuses my composing. With Sibelius or similar software it is easy to start adding lines without thinking very much, just improvising and editing until they fit. With a pencil and paper I have to think far more carefully about the harmony, the counterpoint, and how I am using and developing my ideas.

Having to hear everything in my head, rather than letting the software play me an electronic version, helps as well, believe it or not! I think the "orchestra in your head" is sometimes a more accurate picture of how a piece will sound than the (admittedly excellent) sounds a computer makes.

Writing by hand also makes for much quicker arranging. I print the original piece on a piano staff and annotate it before putting anything into the computer.

Give hand writing music a go!

So if you are an avid user of notation software on your computer or iPad, take a moment to print some manuscript paper, find a pencil and try the old-fashioned way. You may just find you enjoy it!
